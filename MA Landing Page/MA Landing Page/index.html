EOG · Eye-controlled patient assistance

Ask for help
with a glance.

EqualCare lets patients who can’t speak or move call for help with just their eyes. Gel electrodes and a BioAmp Pill read eye movements, an ESP32 sends the request over Wi‑Fi, and the staff dashboard tracks it until it’s resolved.

01 · The problem

Some patients think clearly but can’t say what they need.

53.9%

of 2,671 patients on breathing machines in two US hospitals were awake and alert enough to use a communication aid.

Happ et al., Heart & Lung, 2015

3×

more likely to suffer a preventable harmful event in hospital when a patient has trouble communicating.

Bartlett et al., CMAJ, 2008
Being unable to speak ranks among the most distressing parts of intensive care, alongside thirst, breathlessness and pain.
Brambilla et al., Nursing in Critical Care, 2025

Who struggles to communicate

  • Stroke or brain injuryCan lose speech and the use of one or both arms.
  • ALS / motor neurone diseaseMuscles slowly stop working, but the mind stays sharp.
  • Spinal cord injuryParalysis from the neck down can rule out hands.
  • Patients on a breathing machineA tube in the throat means no voice at all.

What they have today

  • Call bellNeeds a hand that can press a button. A paralysed patient simply can’t use it.
  • Alphabet boards and lip readingSlow and tiring, and only work if a nurse is already at the bedside.
  • Nobody tracks the requestIf a call is missed, it’s forgotten. No one knows how long the patient waited.

The key insight

Eye movement and blinking are often the last movements a paralysed person can still control.

In locked-in syndrome, for example, the person is fully aware, and blinking is how they show it. EqualCare turns that movement into a request.

Sources: Happ et al., Heart & Lung, 2015; Bartlett et al., CMAJ, 2008; Brambilla et al., Nursing in Critical Care, 2025; “Consciousness in the Locked-in Syndrome”, The Neurology of Consciousness, 2009.

02 · How it works

From an eye movement to a nurse at the bedside.

Two deliberate actions are needed, choose and then confirm, so an ordinary blink never sends a request by accident.

  1. EyesThe patient looks or blinks.
  2. SensorGel pads pick up the eye signal.
  3. Mini-computerThe ESP32 checks it was on purpose.
  4. Patient screenShows the chosen request.
  5. Staff screenA nurse sees it and responds.

↩ “Help is coming ✓” goes back to the patient

No hands, no voice

Works for a patient who can’t move their hands or speak.

No helper needed to start

The patient asks on their own, any time, instead of waiting to be noticed.

Never forgotten

Every request is tracked from Pending to Acknowledged to Resolved. If it waits too long, it turns red.

    04 · Training data

    Calibrated on real EOG recordings.

    Every eye movement changes the voltage on the skin around the eye. Here is what that looks like at each stage of the pipeline.

    Illustrative animation, synthetic signal. Recording: [FILL: training video URL]

    1. 1
      Raw EOGThree gel pads (IN+, IN−, REF) pick up a tiny voltage that shifts as the eye moves. It arrives noisy and drifting.
    2. 2
      FilteredThe BioAmp EXG Pill amplifies it thousands of times and filters out noise. The ESP32 reads it about 250 times a second; each movement becomes a clean step and a blink becomes a sharp spike.
    3. 3
      Direction labelsEach deliberate movement is classified and mapped to a command.
